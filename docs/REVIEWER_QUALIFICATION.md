# Reviewer Qualification Standard v0.4

## Principle

Reviewer expertise must match the claim being scored.

The benchmark does not claim "clinical validity" merely because someone has an MBBS.

## Minimum qualification by track

### Medical factual correctness / safety
Primary reviewer:
- licensed physician with completed medical degree (MBBS/MD/DO equivalent), AND
- demonstrated subject familiarity for the assigned domain.

For high-severity items, at least one reviewer must be a postgraduate-trained clinician or documented subject-matter specialist in the relevant discipline.

### Anatomy / Physiology / Biochemistry / Pathology / Pharmacology / Microbiology
Preferred:
- postgraduate qualification in the relevant discipline, OR
- medical degree plus documented teaching/examining experience in that discipline.

### PG question quality
Reviewer:
- medical graduate with direct experience preparing for, teaching, writing, or reviewing postgraduate medical examinations.
- At least one senior reviewer for the final calibration set.

### Concept ontology / relationship annotation
Reviewer:
- medical graduate or relevant biomedical postgraduate,
- plus ontology annotation training using the benchmark handbook.

### English/linguistic quality
Medical reviewers remain responsible for medical meaning. A language specialist may review wording but cannot overturn a medical gold label alone.

## Calibration

Before scoring production items:
- 30-item calibration set per track.
- Reviewers must reach pre-registered agreement with the reference adjudication set.
- Failed calibration triggers retraining/replacement.

## Conflict of interest

Reviewers must not score their own authored items, their own publications, or items they materially contributed to.

## If the qualification bar cannot be met

The benchmark may still be run as a **developmental evaluation**, but it must be labelled:

> EXPERT-QUALIFICATION INSUFFICIENT — NOT VALID FOR PRODUCTION PASS

It cannot produce an official PASS.

This is a hard integrity rule. We never lower reviewer standards to manufacture a passing result.

## Reviewer identity and blinding

The evaluator interface hides:
- candidate name/model family where feasible,
- candidate ranking,
- previous scores,
- other reviewers' labels.

Reviewer IDs are retained privately for audit.
