# For a beta tester who is not the author

Read this before you start. It is short, and the third section is the one that
matters.

## What this is

Quintek turns material you upload — lecture notes, a scanned chapter, a
question bank — into postgraduate-level revision questions, and schedules them
so you see the ones you are weakest on.

The server states its own scope at `GET /scope`, and the app shows it:

> Quintek is a revision aid. Its questions are generated by a language model
> from the material you upload, and they can be wrong — including confidently
> and plausibly wrong. Check anything that matters against a textbook, a
> guideline or a clinician. This is not a clinical reference and must not be
> used to make a decision about a patient.

## Where it is

`https://quintek-demo.onrender.com` — live, running on managed PostgreSQL.

Two things to expect that are not faults:

* **The first request after a quiet period takes about 50 seconds.** The
  service is on a free plan and spins down when idle. It is waking up, not
  broken.
* **The database is on a free plan that expires 2026-10-16.** Anything you put
  in before then goes away with it. This is a beta on purpose.

## Getting started

1. **Register.** Email and a password. No invitation code, no approval step.
   Registration is rate-limited to 5 in a window; a 6th returns `429`.
2. **Make a notebook.** One per subject or per exam.
3. **Add a source.** A PDF, or pasted text. PDFs with a real text layer work;
   scans need OCR, which is not configured, and the app will tell you so
   rather than silently producing nothing — see `GET /capabilities` for what
   this deployment can actually read.
4. **Wait for ingestion.** The source screen shows real progress. If a page
   comes out badly, the app says so instead of quietly using it.
5. **Generate questions**, then revise. Answer, then pick a colour for how
   confident you were — the colour is yours and the system never infers it.

## The part that matters: questions can be wrong

**Nothing has been qualified to generate medical content for you yet.** The
authoritative state is NO MODEL QUALIFIED / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, and while
that holds the app refuses to generate rather than using an unqualified model.
That refusal is correct behaviour. It is not a fault and there is no setting
to work around it.

When generation is switched on, every question will show:

* the **passage it came from**, with its page or locator;
* **how confident the extraction was**, and whether that chunk was flagged as
  needing review — a blurry scan produces a low number and you will see it;
* **what the validator said** about the question.

If a question is wrong, say so — from the question itself, or
`POST /questions/{id}/reports`. Pick a kind (`factually_wrong`,
`wrong_answer_keyed`, `not_in_source`, `ambiguous`, `offensive_or_unsafe`,
`other`) and add a note. Reports asserting a factual error queue for
adjudication by two named clinicians; the rest are read and answered.

Reporting is the single most useful thing you can do. The validator lowers the
rate of wrong questions and nothing lowers it to zero, so the remainder is
found by whoever it misleads. Better that they get reported than believed.

## Your data

* **Export it** any time: `GET /account/export` returns everything held about
  you, as JSON. It excludes your password hash.
* **Delete it** — **CURRENTLY BROKEN, and you should know before you start.**
  `DELETE /account` works for an account that has not yet answered a question.
  Once you have recorded a single attempt it returns a 500 and deletes
  nothing. The cause is a deliberate rule that attempts cannot be removed
  (they are the evidence every colour and priority is derived from) colliding
  with this promise, and resolving it is a decision about what "delete my
  data" should mean here rather than a bug with an obvious fix. It is written
  up as ADR-029.

  Until it is resolved: **assume anything you put in cannot be taken out.**
  Do not upload a source you would mind leaving behind, and if you want your
  account removed, ask — it can be done directly against the database.

  What the operation does when it succeeds: it removes your uploaded files
  too, verifies that nothing is left, and fails loudly rather than reporting a
  deletion it did not complete. That verification is what caught this.
* Two things survive, with your id removed rather than the row: incident
  records (evidence that the system failed — a fault should not disappear
  because the person who hit it left) and any question reports you filed (a
  wrong question does not stop being wrong).
* Your account can be suspended by an administrator — reversibly, with a
  recorded reason and a named person who made the decision. Suspension keeps
  your data; it does not delete it.

## What is not built

See `docs/NOT_BUILT.md`. It is a real list, kept honest on purpose. Do not
assume a missing feature is a bug you have found — check there first, and
report it if it is not listed.

## Reaching someone

Bugs and wrong questions: use the in-app report. It carries the question's full
provenance automatically, which makes it far more useful than a description.

Anything else — or anything that worries you — goes to the author directly.
